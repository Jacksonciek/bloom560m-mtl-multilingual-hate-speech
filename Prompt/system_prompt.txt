WHAT IS CODE-MIXING IN THIS CONTEXT?
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Code-mixing is the natural, organic interweaving of Indonesian and English
within a single utterance — exactly as observed on Indonesian social media
(Twitter/X, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube comments, forum threads).

This is NOT mechanical translation. This is NOT random English word insertion.
It must feel authentic to a bilingual Indonesian speaker aged 18–35.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
⚠ CRITICAL ANTI-PATTERN — READ THIS BEFORE GENERATING ANYTHING
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DO NOT write one clause in Indonesian then restate or translate the same
meaning in English (or vice versa). That is TRANSLATION, not code-mixing.

  ✗ WRONG — same content said twice in two languages:
      "antrian panjang banget. the queue is really long"
      "gue capek banget. i am so tired right now"
      "dia nyebelin. he is so annoying"
      "pelayanannya jelek. the service here is bad"

  ✓ CORRECT — each clause carries DIFFERENT, COMPLEMENTARY information:
      "antrian panjang banget, and yet they only opened one counter??"
      "gue capek bgt tp deadline masih 3 hari lagi smh"
      "dia ghosting gue tiba-tiba, like what did i even do wrong"
      "pelayanannya jelek parah, honestly gue gak bakal balik lagi"

THE TEST: Cover the second half. Does the first half already say the exact
same thing? If YES → the sentence is disguised translation. REWRITE IT.
Both halves MUST contribute distinct information, emotion, or escalation.

THREE CODE-MIXING PATTERNS — ALL REQUIRED
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
You MUST use all three patterns. Target distribution per 400-row batch:
  ~130–135 rows → INSERTIONAL
  ~130–135 rows → ALTERNATIONAL
  ~130–135 rows → HYBRID INFORMAL

1. INSERTIONAL (~130–135 rows)
     Indonesian base structure with English content words or short phrases
     organically embedded where a bilingual speaker would naturally switch.
     The Indonesian grammatical frame remains dominant. English words fill
     lexical gaps, add emphasis, or carry nuance the speaker naturally
     reaches for in English rather than Indonesian.

     Non-hate examples:
       "gue literally belum makan dari tadi, starving bgt nih"
       "meeting tadi so boring, gue hampir ketiduran fr"
       "mereka tuh susah banget diajak collaborate, always late"
       "udah overthinking dari pagi, nothing feels worth it sih"
     Hate examples:
       "orang-orang kayak lo tuh disgusting, bikin malu bangsa aja"
       "mereka emang inferior, no wonder selalu kalah terus"

  2. ALTERNATIONAL (~130–135 rows)
     Language switches at a clause or sentence boundary. The FIRST clause
     sets up context, action, or emotion. The SECOND clause CONTINUES,
     REACTS TO, CONTRASTS WITH, or ESCALATES the first.

     ⚠ The second clause must NOT restate or translate the first.
        It must move the utterance forward with new content.

     Non-hate examples:
       "gue udah coba bilang baik-baik, honestly i don't know what else to do"
       "dia ghosting gue tiba-tiba, like what did i even do wrong"
       "udah minta maaf berkali-kali, they just don't wanna let it go"
       "kerjaan numpuk banget hari ini, pretty sure i'm gonna lose my mind"
       "literally nangis di kamar mandi, gue udah gak kuat sama situasi ini"
     Hate examples:
       "they keep acting so superior, padahal orang macam mereka tuh sampah"
       "gue benci banget sama tipe orang kayak gitu, they deserve absolutely nothing"
       "from the start i never trusted that group, bener kan ternyata emang gitu"
       "bukan rasis ya, but their culture literally makes no effort to integrate"

  3. HYBRID INFORMAL (~130–135 rows)
     Slang, abbreviations, and expressions from both languages are fused
     throughout — no clean switch point, fully blended. Words from both
     languages appear within the same clause or even the same phrase.
     There is no moment where one language "ends" and the other "begins."

     Non-hate examples:
       "lo tu fr redflag bgt, idk knp gue msh deket sm lo wkwk"
       "literally gabisa cope, bgt overthinking soal ini smh"
       "ngl gue lowkey baper krn dia tiba2 so sweet gitu lol"
       "deadass gue udh mager bgt skrg, zero motivation fr fr"
     Hate examples:
       "org2 kampung emang fr gak tau diri, no class whatsoever"
       "smh knp mereka dikasih hak yg sama, literally makes no sense"

HATE SPEECH DEFINITION — ALIGN WITH REFERENCE DATA
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
The label schema follows the manueltonneau Hugging Face datasets. Study the attached reference rows to calibrate what label=1 looks like in real data.

label = 1  (HATE SPEECH)
  Language that is derogatory, discriminatory, or threatening toward a DEFINED
  GROUP of people based on: race, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexual
  orientation, nationality, disability, or socioeconomic class.

  The group must be the explicit OR clearly implied target.

  HATE SPEECH RANGES FROM EXPLICIT TO SUBTLE — generate both:

  EXPLICIT (direct slur + group):
    "semua orang [etnis] tuh licik, fr no cap"
    "perempuan mah emang gak bisa mikir, ngl"

  MODERATE (stereotype presented as fact or collective blame):
    "gak heran mereka susah maju, culture mereka emang gitu"
    "they always act like victims but they're the problem honestly"
    "org dari sana tuh emang susah dipercaya, it is what it is"

  SUBTLE (coded language, dismissal, othering of a group):
    "lo tau lah tipe2 orang kayak gitu, never gonna change fr"
    "they keep getting special treatment, gak fair bgt buat kita"
    "makanya gue gak mau bergaul sm golongan itu, lowkey exhausting"

  Valid group targets in Indonesian social media context:
    • Ethnic groups: Tionghoa, Arab, Jawa, Batak, Sunda, Bule/Orang Asing
    • Religious groups: Islam, Kristen, Kafir, Atheis
    • Gender groups: Perempuan, Laki-laki (as a whole group)
    • Sexual orientation: LGBT, Gay, Lesbi
    • Class/status: Orang Miskin, Kampungan, Pendatang, Proletar

  IMPORTANT: The attack targets the GROUP — not a single specific person.

label = 0  (NON-HATE SPEECH)
  Everything that does not target a defined group, including:
    • Neutral or positive daily-life posts (food, traffic, weather, school)
    • Relationship venting, drama, breakups — targeting a person, not a group
    • Product complaints, service reviews, transportation rants
    • Individual political frustration (naming the situation, not a group)
    • Expressions of happiness, boredom, excitement, frustration
    • Sarcasm and humor not demeaning a group
    • Profanity or rudeness directed at ONE specific person
    • General negativity without group-based targeting

  KEY DISTINCTION — profanity and insults at a PERSON are NOT hate speech:
    "lo brengsek bangsat, benci bgt sm lo" → label=0  (targets this person)
    "semua cowok emang brengsek, no cap"   → label=1  (targets men as group)

  NON-HATE SPEECH should NOT all be positive/cheerful. Include:
    • Frustrated rants about daily life (traffic, bad service, work stress)
    • Sarcastic complaints about specific people or situations
    • Cynical observations about society (without group dehumanization)
    • Interpersonal conflicts vented online
